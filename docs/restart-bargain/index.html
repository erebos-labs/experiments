The restart bargain

Starting over can lower the average search time while delaying the halfway point. Change the restart rule and see who benefits.

Choose the search

A walker moves left or right with equal chance on sites 0 to 40. Site 0 is the target. An attempted step past 40 stays at 40.

Numeric search over reset chances 0 to 0.25. This optimizes the average; it does not optimize every deadline.

Try a limiting case

Every reset replaces a movement and consumes one tick, even at the starting site. Extra reset ticks are spent waiting; no movement or reset decision occurs during that wait.

The model and its limits

For a blind reset chance r, each active tick resets to the original starting site with chance r; otherwise it makes a fair nearest-neighbor step. The target absorbs permanently. The right wall reflects by holding an outward attempt in place. All comparisons use the same finite corridor, so the baseline mean is finite.

The selective policy applies that reset chance only from sites strictly greater than the starting site. At zero extra cost it cannot make completion later under a common-step coupling: a reset returns to a site no farther right than a normal one-step move could reach. The proof does not cover a return delay. Position sensing, energetic cost, drift, unknown targets, physical travel time, and other search strategies are outside this model.

Calculation is deterministic probability propagation and a tridiagonal linear solve. Tables use every tick; charts draw every fourth tick, so a very small parity oscillation near a crossing can be visually smoothed. The low-mean button uses a finite grid and local refinement; it is numerical evidence, not a proof of the global optimum. A displayed small mean reduction should not be generalized to real-world search without checking those assumptions.

An exact small counterexample

On sites 0 to 10, start at 5 and reset with chance 1/32. Independent rational arithmetic gives a mean below 80 ticks and a median of 51 ticks; without reset, the mean is 80 and the median is 49. The exact certificate includes the mean fraction and probabilities immediately on either side of the median thresholds. The displayed L = 40 case also shows the tradeoff.

Scientific provenance

Stochastic resetting, its mean-time optimum, position-dependent reset rules, and differences between quantile and mean objectives are established research topics. This finite teaching model and its checks are an implementation and exploration, not a claim of scientific novelty.

  • Evans & Majumdar (2011), Diffusion with Stochastic Resetting — foundational Brownian model.
  • Evans & Majumdar (2011), Diffusion with Optimal Resetting — position-dependent reset rules.
  • Belan (2019 preprint; 2020 journal), Median and Mode in First Passage under Restart — quantile and deadline objectives.
  • Radice (2022), Non-homogeneous random walks with stochastic resetting — discrete-time renewal and moment criteria.
