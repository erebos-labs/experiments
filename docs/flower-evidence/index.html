Flower-hearing evidence bench

Does a flower have an ear—or only a provocative result?

Laser measurements detected petal vibration during low-frequency playback, and nectar measured three minutes later was more concentrated. The remarkable adaptive story is possible. It is not yet the only explanation.

    The 2020 technical comment reanalysed the flower-level records and reported weak or absent treatment effects on sugar mass, plus inconsistent concentration effects. The original authors replied that concentration is more relevant to pollinator choice and noted significant vibration × time interactions.

    3 · Stop climbing when the evidence stops

    1. 1

      Petal vibration was measured

      Laser vibrometry recorded stronger vibration at low frequencies and reduced vibration after petal removal.

      measured
    2. 2

      Nectar concentration differed after three minutes

      The original paper reports higher means after bee-like/low playback across four experiments. The later reanalysis disputes consistency, not every concentration interaction.

      reported
    3. 3

      More sugar was delivered

      This depends on nectar volume, density, repeated measures, and the target model. The published reanalysis found weak or no sugar-mass effects.

      disputed
    4. 4

      The response improves plant fitness

      The 2019 paper called functionality a future test. The reply supplied arguments and new field nectar-volume observations, not a direct sound → visitor choice → seed-set experiment.
