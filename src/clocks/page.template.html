What is being mapped?

32 dimensionless phase oscillators use the a Kuramoto-style phase-only toy equation, with K = 1, seed = 7, no noise, and RK4 step 0.025. An upward crossing of an integer multiple of 2π triggers a note. Eight clocks (0, 4, …, 28) are voiced; six become a D-minor pluck palette, two become short metallic sine pings. The clock phase selects onset, not pitch.

One natural cycle (2π model units) becomes one quarter-note beat: seconds = model time × (60 / 130) / (2π). Raw mode keeps interpolated crossing times. Quantized mode rounds those onsets to the nearest sixteenth note; it may shift an onset by up to 57.7 ms. MIDI adds tick rounding at 480 ticks per quarter note, even in raw mode. Grid-rounded onsets at beat 32 are omitted; MIDI note-offs are clamped at that boundary, while WAV keeps a short decay tail.

This is an artistic mapping of a phase-only model, not a biologically accurate firefly simulation, a physical metronome, or evidence that nature has a musical tempo. The eight-bar horizon is a composition boundary. The open chain needs much longer than this short excerpt to align fully. Synthesis uses no recordings or copyrighted samples.

Timing, limits and sources

Audio uses the Web Audio clock and 120 ms lookahead, independently of drawing. Missed onsets after a stalled timer are skipped rather than replayed in a burst. Hiding this tab pauses the pass. Resume cancels and requeues future notes; a paused note’s tail is cut. Voices end within 220 ms, with a live cap of 24. The simple dry patch has no reverb or feedback.

The synchronization ideas are established, not new discoveries here. See Steven H. Strogatz (2000), From Kuramoto to Crawford for the broader phase-oscillator framework. Our ring/open/complete toy and musical mapping are not a reproduction of a biological experiment. Technical references: Web Audio specification and Standard MIDI files.
