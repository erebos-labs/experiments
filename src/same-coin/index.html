What changes?
For HHT versus THH, HHT wins the race exactly when the sequence starts HH. Otherwise, the first eventual HH follows a tail and completes THH first. With a fair coin, those initial HH flips have probability 1/4.
For a fixed number of flips, subtracting the two pattern counts makes the interior terms cancel. Only the first and last HH indicators remain. With a fair coin and at least four flips, each pattern has a 3/16 chance to outscore the other, and a tie has probability 5/8.
Assumptions, method, and primary sources
All calculations assume independent, identically distributed coin flips and empty starting history. First-arrival races have no time cap. Fixed-horizon ties are retained. The displayed decimals are rounded; the fractions use integer arithmetic.
Race results use weighted suffix/prefix overlaps, checked separately against an exact absorbing state machine. Fixed-horizon scores use an exact dynamic program over the last two flips and score difference. This is a mathematical model, with no measured physical coin data.
  • Daniel Felix (2006), Optimal Penney Ante Strategy via Correlation Polynomial Identities — overlap direction and fair-coin strategy.
  • Joshua B. Miller, Penney’s Game Odds From No-Arbitrage, v3 — weighted race probabilities and mean duration.
  • Basdevant, Hénard, Maurel-Ségala & Singh (2025), On cases where Litt’s game is fair — fixed-horizon score symmetry; its footnote identifies this boundary-dependent family.
