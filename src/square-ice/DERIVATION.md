# Counting square ice without pretending the boundary disappears

This is an idealized arrow model, not a measurement or quantitative model of water ice. Every internal vertex has four incident edges and must have two arrows in and two out. All valid configurations have weight 1. The lattice has n×n internal vertices; exterior edges are included when boundary conditions are fixed or free.

## Local recurrence and what was counted

Coordinates increase right/down. Edge bits 1 mean right/down. At one vertex let l,u,r,d be the bits on the left, upper, right and lower edges. The incoming-arrow count is

    l + u + (1-r) + (1-d).

The ice rule is therefore l+u=r+d. A row is generated by fixing the upper bits and the left bit, choosing the right bit at each vertex, then setting d=l+u-r. Keep only d∈{0,1}. This generates each horizontal/vertical row assignment once. For periodic rows, sum over left=right=0 and left=right=1. The matrix entry can be 2 (two distinct constant horizontal arrow rows), so treating it as a Boolean adjacency would undercount.

For an n×n torus the exact count is trace(Tⁿ), with arbitrary-precision integers. The sum of vertical bits is conserved across a periodic row, giving smaller independent blocks. Width2 has distinct parallel edges, not a simple square graph. Width1 is deliberately excluded from the torus comparisons.

For the fixed domain-wall convention used here: top bits all1, bottom bits all0, left bits all0, right bits all1. For polarized boundaries all four bit lists are1. The latter forces every interior bit1, by induction scanning from the upper-left vertex, hence exactly one state.

Independent checks enumerate every edge assignment on the 2×2 and3×3 torus, and every interior assignment under three fixed boundaries through n=3. The full 4×4 torus state/move graph is also constructed. The C++ loop representation below provides a second exact counting method through5×5 periodic and6×6 free grids.

## Why domain-wall arrows count alternating sign matrices

Define A[r,c]=h[r,c]-h[r,c-1], using the exterior left bit0. The local rule also gives A[r,c]=u[r,c]-d[r,c]. Thus A entries are−1,0,1. Horizontal partial sums are bits, begin0 and end1; vertical partial sums also begin0 and end1. Every nonzero row/column sequence consequently alternates +1,−1,+1,…,+1. Conversely those partial sums reconstruct every edge. This proves the bijection in our convention.

The product is credited to Zeilberger in Kuperberg’s Theorem 1; Kuperberg gives another proof using square ice. The published product is

    A(n) = ∏[j=0..n−1] (3j+1)! / (n+j)!.

Primary source: [Kuperberg, theorem 1 and state-sum/ASM correspondence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9712207v1). His arrows are globally reversed from ours; this is a bijection preserving counts. The product was checked against row-transfer results through n=10 by the included checks; evaluating the product itself is not an independent enumeration.

Stirling's formula in this product gives the following leading coefficient of log A(n)/n². The log n terms and the linear terms cancel:

    ∫₀¹ [3x log(3x) − (1+x) log(1+x)] dx
       = (3/2)log3 − 2log2
       = log(3√3/4).

Thus domain-wall effective choices per vertex approach3√3/4≈1.299038. [Lieb's original abstract](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.162.162) gives the periodic limit (4/3)^(3/2)≈1.539601. Their ratio simplifies to32/27. The entropy-density difference is log(32/27)≈0.169899 nats per vertex. These are distinct thermodynamic-limit results, not something proven by our finite sweep. Periodic and fixed grids have different boundary/topology conventions: their finite count ratio is not the probability of a domain-wall event in the torus ensemble.

For the finite uniform domain-wall ensemble, H(global)=log₂ A(n). One-vertex entropy is computed from exact forward/backward completion counts. Summing these marginal entropies overcounts global entropy because the vertices are correlated. It is not a local thermodynamic entropy-density map.

At the upper-left corner, type(l,u,r,d)=(0,1,1,0) forces its row and column; deletion leaves an(n−1)×(n−1) ASM. Its probability is exactly A(n−1)/A(n). This gives an independent check on the computed corner marginal. It is small but nonzero for every finite n>1, so “almost frozen” should not become “strictly frozen.”

## Two changed exterior arrows and a macroscopic path

Start from polarized boundaries and the all-right/all-down state. Reverse only the exterior left arrow of row r and the exterior bottom arrow of column c. Every changed interior edge, read in its original right/down direction, must have balanced in/out flow except at these two boundary endpoints. This directed grid is acyclic. A single unit of integer flow cannot split without a second input, so the changed edges form precisely one monotone path from(r,0) to(n−1,c). No detached directed cycles can exist in this acyclic graph.

The number of states is therefore

    binomial(c+n−1−r, c).

All 385 choices of(n,r,c) for n=1..10 were checked against transfer counts. At n=14, r=0,c=13, two exterior flips create10,400,600 possible paths. Every one changes only O(n) edges; the path can span the sample. Its total entropy grows O(n), so its entropy per vertex still vanishes as n grows. It is a useful middle case between one polarized state and the extensive domain-wall family.

## The independent-vertex estimate and an exact loop correction

With independent random edge signs, a single four-valent vertex is valid with probability6/16=3/8. On a torus there are2V edges, V=n² vertices, giving the independence estimate

    2^(2V) (3/8)^V = (3/2)^V.

Here is an independently derived finite identity explaining its failure; no novelty claim is made. Let s₁,…,s₄∈{−1,+1} denote signs pointing into a vertex. The exact valid-vertex indicator is

    I(s) = 3/8 − (1/8)∑[i<j] sᵢsⱼ + (3/8)s₁s₂s₃s₄
         = (3/8)[1 − (1/3)∑[i<j]sᵢsⱼ + s₁s₂s₃s₄].

All 16 inputs were checked using rational arithmetic. Expand the product over vertices and average independent edge signs. Every selected edge must be selected at both endpoints or neither; the surviving subgraphs have degree 0,2 or4 at each vertex. Each selected edge has opposite incidence signs at its endpoints. With n₂ degree 2 vertices and n₄ degree 4 vertices, E_selected=n₂+2n₄, so the edge-sign factor cancels the(−1)^n₂ factors. Every surviving term is positive:

    Z_torus = (3/2)^V ∑[even subgraphs F] 3^(−n₂(F)).

The empty subgraph is the independence term. Closed loops, including winding loops, supply additional terms. Degree4 intersections have coefficient 1; this is not a gas of independent noninteracting polygons. On a free-boundary square, dangling exterior signs integrate out and cannot be selected, giving

    Z_free = 2^(2n) (3/2)^V ∑[even subgraphs of internal grid] 3^(−n₂(F)).

There are2^(E−V+1) even subgraphs of a connected graph. The C++ program verifies a GF(2)-independent cycle basis, then enumerates all its XOR combinations in Gray-code order. It sums integers3^(V−n₂), avoiding floating point. Periodic bases use all but one plaquette plus two winding cycles; the free grid uses its interior plaquettes. Unsigned128-bit arithmetic has ample headroom at the explicit bounds; larger grids are rejected.

For just four junctions in a square with eight free exterior arrows, V=4. The independence estimate is81. There is exactly one nonempty even subgraph, its four-edge loop, so the answer is81(1+1/81)=82. Exhaustive enumeration of all4096 edge assignments verifies that every proper subset of the four vertex-validity events is independent, but the joint event has82 assignments rather than81. Pairwise checks alone miss the dependence.

## Why square flips cannot sample the whole torus

Define Qx as right-minus-left arrow count across a vertical cut and Qy as down-minus-up count across a horizontal cut. The ice rule makes each independent of cut location. Reversing a directed elementary plaquette preserves both. Therefore a chain of plaquette flips cannot cross these flux sectors.

The complete4×4 graph contains2970 states,8768 undirected flip edges and69 components. There are60 isolated frozen states, lying on the saturated-flux boundary. Nine interior flux sectors are each connected at this finite size: center990 states; four adjacent340; four diagonals140. This finite observation is not a proof of general-sector connectivity or a mixing-time bound. [Liu's2018 paper, p3 remark and pp4–5](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol116-approx-random2018/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2018.52/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2018.52.pdf) explicitly distinguishes periodic non-irreducibility from other boundary cases.

Our domain-wall examples instead use exact completion weights. If B(r,a) counts completions from row-state a, choose each next row with probability B(r+1,b)/B(r,a). These probabilities telescope to1/Z for each complete state. Integer tickets avoid rounding the choice weights. Six seeded outputs were checked vertex by vertex and converted to valid ASMs. This is sampling configurations, not simulating physical dynamics.

## A strong preference is different from a strict rule

For a separate authored toy model, give the six ice vertices weight 1 and all ten other patterns weight q∈[0,1]. The frontier contraction sums over every internal edge assignment with fixed exterior arrows. For q=0 it agrees with hard-ice counts; for q=1 it equals2^[2n(n−1)] for every boundary. Independent n=2,3 defect-count polynomials check fractional q values.

For any fixed q>0, compare two fixed exterior boundaries while pairing identical internal assignments. They can change local weights only at at most4n−4 boundary vertices for n≥2, and at the single vertex for n=1. Each local ratio lies between q and1/q, hence

    |log Z_B − log Z_C| / n² ≤ max(1,4n−4)log(1/q)/n² → 0.

This is a direct bound, not an extrapolation from n≤16. For q=0 the bound is unavailable; polarized and domain-wall entropy densities differ by log(3√3/4). The limits q→0 and n→∞ therefore do not commute for their difference. This model isolates the role of strictly forbidden configurations without making claims about actual defect energies or equilibrium times in water ice.

## Numerical and fixture contract

The soft partition function uses exact integer arithmetic at q=0 and q=1. For 0<q<1 it rejects inputs when n² log(q) is below log(sys.float_info.min), a conservative bound that keeps every assignment weight within the normal binary64 range. It does not report an underflowed positive partition as zero. The included sweep is limited to n≤8 and does not prove exactness of interior floating-point results. The mathematical positivity/bound arguments are separate from numerical precision.

The six n=14 domain samples use Python Random seed 1967 and can be reconstructed with generate_fixtures.py. Canonical loop rows omit nondeterministic seconds; raw timing belongs in output/. Counting entry points validate before shifts/allocations: row/domain widths and heights≤14, free widths≤10, periodic widths≤6. These are finite experiment limits, not a promise that all upper-bound cases are fast or low-memory.
