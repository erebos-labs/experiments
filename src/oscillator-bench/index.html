A model with room
to go quiet.

A phase-only model tracks where each rhythm is in its cycle. This Stuart–Landau model also tracks amplitude, so the oscillations can shrink toward rest.

żᵢ = (1 + iωᵢ − |zᵢ|²)zᵢ + K(zⱼ − zᵢ)

With K = 6, the two amplitudes settle near 0.442891 and the phase difference approaches 30°. They share a frequency without moving in exact unison.

Assumptions & numerical limits

All quantities are dimensionless. Intrinsic angular frequencies are fixed at 2 and 8; growth is 1; coupling is symmetric, instantaneous and linear. There is no noise, delay, escapement or moving platform.

Every run starts at z₁ = 1 + 0.2i and z₂ = −0.3 + 0.7i. The solver is fourth-order Runge–Kutta with step 0.01, bounded to 120 time units. Drawing uses stored samples every 0.05 units; playback speed does not change the integration step.

Close to K = 1 or K = 5, settling can be very slow. A finite run cannot establish a sharp transition, and timestep error can change the apparent sign of an extremely small growth rate. Phase is hidden when either amplitude is below 10⁻⁶ because phase at zero amplitude is undefined.

The stability label comes from the analytic eigenvalues at the origin, not from a visual threshold. “Show outcome” means the end of this bounded run, not a proof about infinite time.

What about real metronomes?

This is a generic oscillator model, not a calibrated metronome. In mechanical experiments, a metronome can stop ticking when its swing no longer engages its escapement. It may still move passively, driven by another metronome. That separate mechanism is context here; it is not simulated by this page.
